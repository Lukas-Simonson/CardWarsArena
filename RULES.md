# Card Wars Rule Book

## Overview

Welcome to the Land of Ooo, where you can play the magical game Card Wars! It's a favorite game of Jake and Finn and many others, and soon it will be your favorite, too.

**Adventure Time Card Wars** is a "Lane Combat" trading card game where the cards in your deck battle against the cards in your opponent's deck. The epic battles all take place on a playing field of 4 Lanes, just like on the show. You can jump right in with a preconstructed deck or you can customize your own!

These rules assume you are playing a 2-player game with preconstructed decks. This is the recommended way to play if you are new to **Card Wars**. Check out [[#Customize Your Deck]] for instructions on constructing your own.
## Objective

Each player begins with 25 hit points (HP). This is the maximum HP you can have (meaning you cannot normally heal above it), and any damage you take reduces this total.

**The object of the game is to reduce your opponent's HP from 25 to 0. Do that and you are the Cool Guy!**

## Setup Sequence

Setup is easy! Just follow the steps below:

1. [[#1. Choose a deck and its Hero|Choose a deck and its Hero]]
2. [[#2. Place your Landscapes on the table|Place your Landscapes on the table]]
3. [[#3. Set your HP total to 25|Set your HP total to 25]]
4. [[#4. Decide who goes first|Decide who goes first]]
5. [[#5. Shuffle and draw 5 cards|Shuffle and draw 5 cards]]
6. [[#6. Take a mulligan *(optional)*|Take a mulligan *(optional)*]]

### 1. Choose a deck and its Hero

Both players choose a deck to play. Each deck is made up of 40 cards and has a specific Hero associated with it. You can learn which Hero goes with which deck by checking out the Hero icons.

### 2. Place your Landscapes on the table

Four large Landscape tiles accompany each deck. To set up the play area, simply place your Landscape tiles in a row, side by side, on the table. Then your opponent does the same opposite your tiles. This creates a battlefield of 4 Lanes.

> [!tip] Lanes
> A single Lane is made up of 2 Landscapes that are opposite each other. The blue box is surrounding one of the 4 Lanes.

### 3. Set your HP total to 25

In a standard game, both players start with their oversized Hero card's HP (round on the back of the card) set to 25. You can either use the included Hero tokens or choose another method to track your hit points.

### 4. Decide who goes first

The Cool Guy (the previous winner) goes first. If this is your first game, determine the first player at random.

### 5. Shuffle and draw 5 cards

Shuffle your cards and place the deck face down to the left or right of your Landscapes. Then, draw the top 5 cards of your deck. Now that you have some cards in your hand, let's look at the card anatomy.

### 6. Take a mulligan *(optional)*

If you decide that you don't like your hand of cards, you can choose to take a "mulligan." To take a mulligan, return your 5 starting cards to your deck, reshuffle all 40 cards together, and then draw a new hand of 5 cards, Keep in mind that you may only do this once at the start of each game.

You are now ready to start the game!

> [!important]
> The first player cannot Floop or Fight during their first turn of the game. Learn more about [[#Flooping]] and [[#Fighting]]

### 1. Ready all of your cards

At the start of your turn, put all of your exhausted Creatures and Buildings into the ready position. Then perform any "start of turn" effects on cards you control or from tokens in any order you choose.

### 2. Draw 1 card

Draw 1 card at no cost (even as the very first player).

> [!important]
> As the game goes on, your hand will shrink or grow; do ***NOT*** refill your hand to 6 cards at the start of each turn.

### 3. Spend up to 2 actions

On each of your turns, you automatically receive 2 actions, but can get more from card effects. Actions are generic and are mostly used to play Creatures, Buildings, and Spells from your hand (see [[#Playing Cards]]). Actions can also be used to activate card abilities. You do not need to spend all of your actions playing cards.

### 4. Floop? *(optional)*

Some Creatures and Buildings have a "FLOOP" ability listed in their game text box. To use a Floop ability, exhaust the card by turning it sideways. For a Creature, move it to the bottom of the Landscape tile, next to the Landscape's name. For a Building, leave it below its Landscape tile.

Once you have properly "Flooped" your card, complete the action listed on the card. It remains Flooped until the start of your next turn when it is made ready along with the rest of your Creatures.

You can choose to Floop before spending your ctions, after spending 1 action, or after spending both actions (before you Fight). You may Floop a Creature or Building in the same turn that it enters play. You may only Floop during your own turn. A Flooped Creature does not Fight at the end of your turn, which is shown by its exhausted state. However, a Flooped Creature will still defend a Land when your opponent Fights. If you would rather attack with a Creature who has a Floop ability, just don't use the Floop ability and it will Fight instead. You are never required to use a Floop ability.

### 5. Spend actions to draw extra cards

You can spend actions to draw extra cards at a cost of 1 action per card drawn. You can do this before or after spending actions to play cards. There is no hand size limit in the game. You do not lose the game if your deck runs out of cards; if you can't draw a card, nothing else happens. Any unspent actions are lost at the end of your turn.

### 6. Fight!

After you have spent your actions and are done Flooping, all of your ready Creatures must Fight (see [[#Fighting]])! You select the order in which your Creatures Fight, so choose wisely. When a Creature Fights, exhaust it and place it at the top of the Landscape, near your opponent's Landscape, to distinguish it from a Flooped Creature.

### 7. End of turn

Your turn is over after your Creatures Fight. Perform any "end of turn" effects and then your opponent's turn begins.

## Playing Cards

To play most cards, you must first make sure you control the matching Landscape types. To "control" something means that it is in play on your side of the play area. All Creatures, Buildings, and Landscapes that are on your side of the play area are under your control. Since spells do not enter play, you can never control a Spell. The phrase "Your Creature" refers to a Creature you control.

When you play a Creature or Building, it comes into play ready.

### Creatures

Each Creature has an action cost and Landscape type. For example, Pit Python has an action cost of 2, which means you need to spend 2 of your actions to put the card into play. Additionally, there is a Useless Swamp symbol in the upper-right corner of the card; that means you must control at least 2 Useless Swamp Landscapes in order to play it. Spending the 2 actions allows you to put the Pit Python into play by placing it, in any Lane, on ANY one of your Landscapes.

> [!important]
> You may play creatures onto any Landscape you control, not just ones that match their Landscape type.

Once you have placed a Creature on a Landscape, the Creature stays there indefinitely until it is destroyed, an effect is used to move/remove it, or you replace it.

If you wish to play a Creature on a Landscape where you already control a Creature, you must replace the Creature that is there. You cannot replace a Creature that is Flooped; it must be ready to be replaced. When you replace a Creature, discard the card to its owner's discard pile and put any tokens on it back into the supply.

> [!tip]
> When you are low on hit points, replacing a Creature with a lot of damage on it might save your bacon! If you lose a Creature during your turn, your opponent's Creature will be able to attack you directly during their turn. By replacing your damaged Creature, you can defend yourself better.

### Buildings

Each Building has an action cost and Landscape type, just like a Creature. For example, Temple of the Torch has an action cost of 1, which means you need 1 action to put it into play. Additionally, there is a LavaFlats symbol in the upper-right corner of the card; that means you must control at least 1 LavaFlats Landscape in order to play it. Spending the 1 action allows you to put the Temple of the Torch into play by placing it, in any Lane, ***BELOW*** one of your Landscapes. It does not matter whether a Creature is on the Landscape or not.

> [!important]
> You may play Buildings below any Landscape you control, not just ones that match their Landscape type

Buildings cannot be attacked and do not normally take damage, but they can be destroyed. Just like Creatures, you can replace a ready Building you control by playing another Building in the same Lane.

### Spells

Each Spell also has an action cost and Landscape type. Just like Creatures and Buildings, you must control the matching Landscapes and spend the listed action cost to play Spells. When you play a spell, read the game text to your opponent, do what it tells you to do, and then put it into its owner's discard pile. Spells are never placed onto a Landscape, Creature, or Building unless an effect says otherwise.

### Rainbow Cards

Rainbow cards may be played using any type of face-up Landscape or combination of Landscapes. This is shown by a special rainbow symbol in the upper-right corner instead of a normal Landscape type. The card type text, below the card art, will also say "Rainbow." For example, Titanbane Amulet is a Rainbow Spell card with a cost of 2; spending 2 actions allows you to play it simply by having 2 face-up Landscapes (such as a Cornfield and Blue Plains).

### Cost 0 Cards

Cost O cards do not require any actions to play. They can even be played after spending your 2 actions (before Fighting). They may only be played during your turn.

Cards that cost 0 and cards that have their cost reduced to 0 by other effects that have a Landscape type still require you to control at least 1 Landscape of that type in order to play them.

Rainbow 0-cost cards don't require you to have a specific Landscape type in play and can be played even it all of your Landscapes are face down.

## Fighting

When you exhaust a Creature to Fight, look at your opponent's side of the Lane. If they do not have a Creature on their side of the Lane, the opposing Landscape is empty and your Creature deals damage to your opponent! When this happens, your opponent reduces their hit points by the value of your Creature's Attack (ATK).

If a Creature is on the other side of the Lane, both damage each other simultaneously. (Remember, a Flooped Creature will still defend its Lane.) The attacker damages the defender's Creature equal to the attacker's Attack value, and the defender damages the attacker equal to the defender's Attack value. Use the HP tokens to track how much damage is on a Creature, counting upwards. A Creature is destroyed and placed into its owner's discard pile when it has damage on it equal to or greater than its Defense (DEF) value.

Your turn is over as soon as all of your ready Creatures are exhausted and done Fighting.
## Other Abilities

The game text on a card can include other abilities besides Flooping. Most abilities are always active, These abilities last only as long as that card is in play.

Anything written before the ">>> " is the cost to use the ability. You cannot use an ability if you cannot pay the cost, but choosing to pay the cost to use an ability is always optional. You may use these abilities any number of times during your tum unless they specify otherwise.

Some card abilities have an action cost. The action cost does not require you to control any particular type of Landscape to use the ability. Remember, you only get 2 actions on your turn, so paying an action for an ability means you won't be able to spend it elsewhere. But if you have a spare action, why not use it!

When a Creature or Building's ability affects something "in this Lane," it means the Lane the card occupies.

## Hero Cards

Heroes will give your deck some extra punch! Each deck has a specific Hero associated with it. Make sure your opponent knows what your Hero does before you begin!

Most of the Hero cards have abilities that are always active; if you meet the requirements listed, you get the bonus, even during your opponents turn. Other Heroes may have abilities with a cost that you may use any number of times during your turn unless they specify otherwise. Hero abilities that say "Once per turn" or "Once per game" are always performed on your turn, when you can normally perform an action, unless otherwise stated on the card.

## Attack Tokens

Some **Card Wars** sets have cards that help to beef up your Creature's attack by empowering them with attack tokens! Each attack token on a Creature adds +1 ATK, turning them into a fearsome force on the battlefield. Attack tokens affect your Creature at all times, not just when they attack!

Use the "3" attack side of a token to represent three "1" attack tokens whenever needed to save space. When a Creature with attack tokens leaves play, return all of it's tokens to the supply.

## Burn Tokens

Certain cards and abilities in some **Card Wars** sets can burn Creatures and players. When you burn a target, put a burn token on it; it is now considered "burning." Note that burning a Creature does not change its Landscape type. For example, a Nicelands Creature with a burn token is now a burning NiceLands Creature.

When a burn token is placed on a target (player or Creature), it immediately takes 1 damage. At the start of their controller's turn, any burning Creatures take an additional 1 damage per burn token currently on them. A burned player also takes 1 damage at the start of their turn for each burn token on them. There is no limit to the number of burn tokens that can be placed on a single target. Use the "3" burn side of a token to represent three "1" burn tokens whenever needed to save space. When a Creature with burn tokens leaves play, return all of its tokens to the supply. Watch as your opponents wither under the relentless heat!
## Frozen Tokens

While not in every **Card Wars** set, certain cards can freeze Landscapes. When you freeze a Landscape, put a frozen token on it; this tile is now referred to as a 'frozen Landscape." Note that a frozen Landscape still retains its original Landscape type. For example, if you play a card that puts a frozen token on a Cornfield Landscape, it is now a frozen Cornfield Landscape.

You cannot play a Creature onto a frozen Landscape. However, a Creature can already be there when a frozen token is added—the Creature there just gets a bit chilly! Additionally, a Creature on a frozen Landscape cannot be replaced, though it can be moved (with a card effect or ability) onto or off of a frozen Landscape.

A Landscape may never have more than 1 frozen token on it. You may still target a frozen Landscape to receive another frozen token, but it simply does not gain one.

To remove a frozen token on your side of the play area, you must choose and discard a card from your hand during your turn. Each discarded card will remove 1 frozen token. As soon as the token is removed, you are free to play Creatures onto the Landscape again.

If a Landscape with a frozen token on it is flipped face down, keep the token on the tile. A Landscape tile that is face down can still be frozen.

## Customize Your Deck

You look like the adventurous type! Are you ready to start constructing your own decks?

First, start by choosing your Landscapes. **Card Wars** features many Landscape types to build your deck around, but you must have exactly 4 Landscape tiles—no more, no less. But you don't have to pick 4 matching Landscapes. For example, you could have a deck that uses a Cornfield, Blue Plains, SandyLands, and IcyLands. Just remember that in order to play a Creature, Spell, or Building that costs 2 actions, you have to have 2 face-up Landscapes of that type in play.

Second, choose a deck size and your cards. The minimum deck size is 40 cards, but feel free to go over that if you wish. You aren't required to have the same number of cards as your opponent. You can include a maximum of three of any particular card.

Third, select your Hero. Since Heroes aren't tied to Landscapes, you can choose any one you want!

And with that, you're ready for battle! You can play with your new deck against preconstructed decks or other customized decks.

## Glossary

- **+ATK:** When a Creature has an ATK bonus, it will deal that much additional damage when it is in a Fight.
- **+DEF:** When a Creature has a DEF bonus, it can take that much more damage before it is destroyed.
- **Adjacent:** A Creature that is in a Lane to the immediate left or right of another Creature is adjacent to that Creature. A Creature that is in a Lane to the immediate left or right of a certain Landscape is adjacent to that Landscape. A Building that is in a Lane to the immediate left or right of another Lane is adjacent to that Lane (or a Creature in that Lane). Creatures, Buildings, and Landscapes you control are only adjacent to other cards you control; your cards are never adjacent to your opponent's cards. A Building is not adjacent to a Creature that it shares a Lane with.
- **Changing Landscapes:** A Creature or Building may change Landscapes only if you have a Spell or ability that says it can change Lanes. When changing Lanes, you may not move a Creature into a Lane where you already control a Creature. Likewise, you may not move a Building into a Lane where you already control a Building.
- **Destroy:** When you destroy a Creature or Building, it is placed into its owner's discard pile. The owner of the card is the player whose deck it came from.
- **Discard:** When you are told to discard a card, that card comes from your hand unless otherwise stated. It goes to your discard pile. You get to choose what to discard unless the effect says otherwise.
- **Discard Pile:** Cards that you discard and cards that leave play are placed into your discard pile. The cards in your discard pile should always be face up, and both players may look at them at any time. If you have control of one of your opponent's cards when it is destroyed, it goes to your opponent's discard pile, not yours. 
- **Empty:** A Landscape that has no Creatures on it is "empty." If both Landscapes in that Lane have no Creatures, that Lane is "empty." For example, if you control 3 Creatures, each in a separate Landscape, you control 1 empty Landscape. During a Fight, a Creature that attacks an opposing empty Landscape deals damage to the opponent and reduces their HP.
- **Free:** "Free" reduces the action cost of a card to 0. You must still control at least 1 Landscape of the required type and meet any other listed requirements to play a free card.
- **Facedown Landscapes:** Some effects can flip a Landscape tile face down. A facedown Landscape reduces the total number of that Landscape type that player controls. For example, if you control 2 Blue Plains Landscapes and then one is flipped face down, you control 1 less Blue Plains Landscape and can no longer play cards that require 2 or more Blue Plains. Similarly, if your only Blue Plains Landscape is flipped face down, it does not count as a Blue Plains type for your card effects. Whenever a Landscape tile is flipped face down or face up, any Creature on it remains on the tile. You may still play a Creature onto a facedown Landscape, and you may still play Buildings below a facedown Landscape. Creatures play the same on a facedown Landscape as they do on one that is face up. Some cards can flip a facedown Landscape back over when they leave play, and others have abilities that can flip a Landscape face up again. Some card effects may refer specifically to Landscapes in their facedown state.
- **Friendly:** A friendly Creature is one that you or a teammate controls: it is not a target for Fighting. A friendly player is you or your teammate.
- **Gobble:** When a Creature with Gobble is replaced, place it underneath the Creature that replaced it instead of discarding it. The new Creature adds the ATK and DEF values of the Gobble Creature to its own, as well as gaining any tokens it held, including damage tokens. A Creature with Gobble does not transfer its card text upon replacement. Creatures in a Gobble stack" like this are temporarily considered to be the same card for effects. You may replace a Creature with Gobble with another Creature with Gobble, which can allow you to have more than 2 cards in a Gobble stack. When the top Creature leaves play, any cards underneath it go with it unless otherwise specified. Once the cards in a Gobble stack leave play, they are considered separate cards again.
- **Opponent:** The other player in the game—not one of their Creatures or Buildings.
- **Opposing:** An opposing Creature or Building is one that your opponent controls.
- **Own vs. Control:** Cards that come from your deck are things you own. Creatures and Buildings on your side of the play area are under your control.
- **Target:** Some Spells and abilities want you to choose a Creature, Building, Landscape, or other component to affect. The component you choose to affect is your target. If your card does not have a target, you may always use it, even if you can't perform the function on your card. If you card has a target, you must target something—even your own **card**—unless the card says otherwise. If an ability asks you to target something and there are no choices that fulfill the requirements, you cannot use that ability or Spell.
- **Replace:** The only time you can replace one of your Creatures or Buildings is when a new Creature or Building is entering play. When. you replace one of your Creatures with another Creature in the same Lane, it leaves play and is discarded (not destroyed).
- **Whump:** When you play a Creature with Whump, its cost is temporarily reduced by the cost of the Creature it replaces. If you cannot pay the adjusted cost of a Creature with Whump, you may not play it. Once the Creature with Whump is played, its cost reverts to its original value.

- **Spell Warp:** The 2 Creatures you swap change Lanes simultaneously.
- **Struzann Djinn:** Creatures that Flooped during your turn still count as Flooped Creatures during your opponent’s turn. Be sure to keep your Flooped Creatures near the back of the Landscape so you can tell the difference between Creatures who Flooped and ones who Fight.
- **Subliminal Strength:** This only counts the Spells you have already played this turn.
- **Tapped Out:** This only counts currently exhausted Creatures. Flooped Creatures are exhausted, as well as any Creature that did not ready at the start of your turn.
- **The Dog:** A Building that loses its abilities is considered to have no game text, but it still has a cost, Landscape type, and card type.
- **Tired Wombat:** If there are no empty Landscapes, it remains where it is
- **Tornado Wall of Fire:** Note that the +3 ATK bonus applies while attacking or defending in place of the Pied Piper.
- **Travelin’ Skeleton:** Both of your Creatures swap at the same time.
- **Unempty Coffin:** A Creature’s cost cannot be reduced below 0.
- **Vampire Lord:** If it has 1 DEF remaining and it takes 1 damage and deals 1 damage during a Fight, it lives that round.
- **Wall of Sand:** Remember that Rainbow Creatures do not count as Cornfield, Useless Swamp, SandyLands, or Blue Plains Creatures.
- **Woad Talisman:** Remember that Rainbow Creatures do not count as Cornfield, Useless Swamp, SandyLands, or Blue Plains Creatures.
- **Volcano:** There must be a Building in play to be able to play this card. A facedown Landscape does not have a Landscape type, but Creatures and Buildings may still be placed there. If you flip over a Landscape that you have only two of, you now only have one of that type. You won’t be able to play any more cards of that Landscape type with a 2 action cost. Fortunately, you can discard those cards to some of your Useless Swamp Creatures.
- **Woadic Weirdo:** The card you reveal becomes a Rainbow card temporarily, so you do not need the required Landscape types to play it
- **ZaZo's Magic Seeds:** Remember that Rainbow Creatures do not count as Cornfield, Useless Swamp, SandyLands, or Blue Plains Creatures.
